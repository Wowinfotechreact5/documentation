## AI Content Generator

AI Content Generator helps you turn an idea into a website or blog draft.

Use it when you want to:

- Generate blog articles about your products, services, cities, or expanded FAQs
- Create SEO content that targets a specific keyword and search intent
- Write educational content, such as how-to guides, comparisons, and checklists
- Create content from keyword ideas found in [SEO Intelligence](/seo-intelligence)
- Support your website growth strategy with new content every week
- Turn SEO opportunities into ready-to-publish drafts without waiting for an agency

**How to get a good start:** Pick one keyword, one intent, and one city or audience. For example: "2 BHK cleaning price Baner for homeowners". A vague topic like "best services" produces a generic article, while a specific topic produces an article that can rank.

Each run creates a Blog Project with these stages: research, outline, content, image, SEO, and publish settings. Do not skip any stage.

## Content Creation Flow

A typical content flow looks like this:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose or create a topic">
    Pick a topic from SEO Intelligence keywords, a competitor gap, or a brand prompt. Make sure it does not duplicate an article that is already live.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review AI research results">
    Check the angle, the facts, and the competitors mentioned. Reject the research if it is off topic.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the blog outline">
    Fix the order of the headings and add any missing sections, such as pricing or FAQ.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Edit the article in AI Editor">
    Fix the facts, tone, links, and call to action. A person must always review the article.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add or review the featured image">
    Check that the image is relevant, has alt text, and is the right size.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure publish settings">
    Select the blog subdomain or Cloudflare path, and set the slug, meta details, and schedule.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish or save the content">
    **Publish** makes the article live if your integration shows green. **Save** keeps it as a draft for later.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Do not jump straight from choosing a topic to publishing. Each step improves your ranking and helps you avoid legal risk. On average, reviewing a 1200-word article takes about 20 minutes.
</Warning>

## AI Research Results

AI Research Results let you review the topic before the article is written. This saves you from rewriting later.

Use this step to check whether the article direction is useful for your audience. Read the summary, sources, angle, and the questions covered.

Check the following:

- **Search intent:** Is the topic informational or commercial, and does the research match it?
- **Audience:** Is it written for the right reader, such as homeowners or businesses?
- **Competitor gap:** Is there an angle your competitors miss that you can win?
- **Factual risk:** Are there details like prices or laws that you need to verify?

If the direction is wrong, change the topic or brief and generate again. Fixing it now costs less than fixing a full draft.

## Blog Outline

The Blog Outline step gives the article a structure that works for both readers and Google.

Use it to check that:

- The main headings (H1 and H2) are in a logical order.
- The subtopics answer all the reader's questions, with no thin sections.
- The structure matches the search intent: how-to articles need steps, pricing articles need a table, and comparison articles need pros and cons.
- The content flows from the introduction to the details, then to the FAQ and the call to action, without jumping around.
- No important sections are missing, such as pricing, location, process, risks, FAQ, or call to action.

Always add an FAQ section. Add placeholders for internal links, such as your pricing page and contact page. Remove filler headings that repeat the same point.

[https://portal.wowagent.ai/ai-blog-outline](https://portal.wowagent.ai/ai-blog-outline)

<Warning>
  Approve the outline before you generate the content. A bad outline leads to a bad article, even with good editing.
</Warning>

## AI Editor

The AI Editor is where you review and polish the article. Never publish AI output without editing it first.

Use it to edit:

- **Title:** Keep it under 60 characters, with the keyword and a hook.
- **Headings:** Use the keyword once in the H1 and naturally in the H2s. Do not stuff keywords.
- **Body content:** Verify all facts, numbers, dates, and names.
- **Links:** Add 3 to 5 internal links (for example, to your pricing, service, and contact pages) and 1 to 2 links to trusted external sites.
- **Formatting:** Use short paragraphs, bullet points, tables for prices, and bold text for key points.
- **Tone:** Match the brand tone set in your chatbot, such as friendly or professional.
- **Call to action:** Add a clear call to action in the middle and at the end, such as Call, Book Demo, or Get Quote, with a link and phone number.

Read the article aloud once. If it sounds boring or repetitive, cut about 20 percent. Add a real example that mentions a city and a result.

[https://portal.wowagent.ai/ai-editor-page](https://portal.wowagent.ai/ai-editor-page)

Save your draft often. Check that the word count and reading time match the target in your brief.

## Featured Image

Use the Featured Image settings to review or add the article's main image. Articles without an image get fewer clicks.

Check that the image:

- Is relevant to the topic
- Does not contain too much text
- Is under 200 KB so the page loads quickly (WebP format is preferred)
- Has alt text that includes the keyword and some context
- Has a clean title tag

If an AI-generated image looks fake for a real business, such as a clinic or real estate company, replace it with a real photo. For long articles, add 2 to 3 images inside the article, each with alt text.

[https://portal.wowagent.ai/featured-image-page](https://portal.wowagent.ai/featured-image-page)

## Publish Settings

Publish Settings control where and how the article is published. A wrong setting can leave the draft stuck or cause a 404 error on the live page.

Check the following:

- **Destination:** Choose a blog subdomain (such as blog.example.com) or a Cloudflare path (such as example.com/blog).
- **Slug:** Keep it short and include the keyword.
- **Meta title and description:** Fill in both.
- **Canonical URL:** Set it if the article was published elsewhere before.
- **Schedule:** Set the date, author, and category.

## Content Calendar

Content Calendar helps you plan upcoming content so your team publishes every week instead of at random.

Use it to:

- View scheduled content by week and month
- Plan publishing dates around offers, seasons, and launches
- Plan content campaigns, such as 4 pricing guides in March
- Track the status of upcoming articles: draft, in review, or scheduled
- Keep your team aligned with an owner and due date for each article

**Publishing rule of thumb:** New websites should publish 1 to 2 posts per week, and websites in competitive markets should publish 4 or more. Aim for this mix: 40 percent service and pricing posts, 30 percent how-to posts, 20 percent comparisons, and 10 percent offers. Do not publish 4 offer posts in a row.

If a draft is delayed, move it to the next open slot on the same day. Do not pile 5 articles onto one date.

## Published Content

Published Content shows articles that are already live. Audit them every month.

Use it to:

- Review live articles with their title, URL, and date
- Open article previews to check formatting on mobile
- Check your publishing history for gaps
- Compare the number of articles published against your plan
- Find posts with outdated prices or policies that need refreshing

**Monthly audit (20 minutes):** Open your top 10 posts by traffic and check their prices, phone numbers, links, and images. Update the date after you refresh a post. Add internal links from new posts to your best-performing older posts.

## Before Publishing

AI can make up information, so a person must always review AI-generated content before publishing. Check each of these:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Facts">
    Verify dates, statistics, addresses, and awards against a reliable source.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Pricing">
    Check price slabs, taxes, and extra charges. They must match your Products section.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Claims">
    Remove claims like "Number 1", "guaranteed", or "best" unless you can prove them.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Contact details">
    The phone number, email, and address must match your Business Profile.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Brand voice">
    Keep the tone consistent, whether friendly or professional.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Grammar and spelling">
    Read the article once, and check that any Hindi-English mixed text is correct too.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Internal links">
    Include 3 to 5 working internal links with no 404 errors and the correct link text.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="External links">
    Link only to trusted websites, and open them in a new tab.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Images">
    Make sure images are licensed, have alt text, are the right size, and are not blurry.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Legal or sensitive statements">
    Be careful with refund, medical, or legal advice. If a statement is risky, add a disclaimer and a line telling readers to contact your team.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
  Tick every item on this checklist before you click Publish. A single post with a wrong price can lead to 10 angry calls.
</Warning>

## Best Practices

Use [SEO Intelligence](/seo-intelligence) to choose content topics. Start with high-intent, low-competition keywords instead of high-volume keywords that only look impressive.

Write to answer customer questions, not just to target keywords. A title like "Cost of 2 BHK Cleaning in Baner" works better than "Ultimate Cleaning Guide".

Review every article before publishing. Never let articles publish automatically without a person reviewing them.

Keep your published content up to date. Refresh pricing posts every quarter, and update policy posts on the same day a policy changes.

<Warning>
  Do not publish outdated pricing or policy information. If you are not sure, save the article as a draft and confirm the details with your operations team.
</Warning>

Use internal links to connect related pages. Every new blog post must link to your pricing, service, and contact pages. Your best-performing older posts must link to related new posts.

Review your published articles regularly: every month, after any price or phone number change, and after a website migration to catch broken links.
